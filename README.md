## 📌 Project overview

This was developed as a **core semester project in Electronics and Telecommunication Engineering**. The project studies how a low-voltage rechargeable power source can be managed and converted into short high-voltage pulses using a pulse-generation module and transformer-based step-up stage.

The work combines circuit-level concepts with practical enclosure, switching, charging, insulation, and safety considerations. The accompanying paper discusses the operating principle, system blocks, applications, limitations, and risks of conducted-energy devices.

> **Academic prototype:** This repository documents an educational electronics project. It is not a medical device, law-enforcement device, or recommendation for weapon construction or use.

## 🧠 Engineering concepts explored

- Electromagnetic induction and voltage step-up
- High-voltage pulse generation from a low-voltage source
- Rechargeable lithium-ion power management
- Protected single-cell charging
- SPST power switching and momentary activation
- LED-based power/status indication
- Insulation, enclosure design, and electrical hazard awareness
- High-voltage measurement and responsible laboratory practice

## 🧩 High-level system blocks

```text
Rechargeable power source
        ↓
Protected charging and power-management stage
        ↓
Main switch + momentary activation control
        ↓
High-voltage pulse-generation stage
        ↓
Transformer-based step-up output
        ↓
Insulated enclosure and controlled demonstration
```

The repository intentionally keeps this diagram conceptual. Detailed wiring, operating parameters, and construction instructions are not provided because high-voltage circuits can cause severe injury, fire, or death.

## ⚠️ Safety and responsible-use notice

This project involves **potentially lethal high voltage** and rechargeable lithium-ion batteries. Do not reproduce, modify, test, or operate the circuit without qualified supervision, suitable high-voltage instruments, proper isolation, protective equipment, and compliance with local laws and institutional safety procedures.

- Never test an electrical device on a person or animal.
- Never handle, probe, or modify an energized high-voltage circuit.
- Never rely on a visible spark as proof that a circuit is safe.
- Keep lithium-ion cells protected from short circuits, overheating, and physical damage.
- Treat all capacitors and high-voltage sections as charged until professionally discharged and verified.
- Use the project for academic study and safety-focused electronics learning only.
